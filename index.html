Do International Students and Language Enrolments Follow Similar Trends?

Explore how international school enrolments and language subject enrolments in NSW changed between 2012 and 2024.

Both series are indexed to 100 in 2012. Similar trends do not establish a causal relationship.

Key Insight

International school enrolments and language subject enrolments followed noticeably different trends in NSW. International enrolments rose strongly before 2020, declined sharply during 2020–2022, and began recovering afterwards. In contrast, language enrolments remained relatively stable before declining moderately in later years. This suggests that changes in international school enrolments do not necessarily correspond with changes in language subject participation.

Note: Language enrolment data for 2022 is unavailable. Both datasets are indexed to 100 in 2012. Similar trends do not establish a causal relationship.

THE FINAL PICTURE

Beyond the Numbers

Language learning in NSW government schools is not defined by a single trend. Different languages lead at different school levels, and enrolment patterns continue to change over time.

01

Different Classrooms, Different Leaders

In 2024, Mandarin recorded the highest primary-school language enrolments, while Japanese led secondary-school enrolments in Years 7–9.

02

Popularity Does Not Guarantee Growth

A language can remain widely studied while experiencing declining enrolments. Comparing enrolment counts and percentage changes provides a more complete understanding of language-learning trends.

03

Geography Adds Another Perspective

Government schools are distributed across metropolitan, regional and rural NSW. Their locations provide useful geographical context, although they do not directly measure language-learning opportunities or participation.

Understanding the Limitations

The language enrolment data covers 2012–2024, while the school location data represents 2026. These datasets describe different aspects of NSW education and should not be interpreted as directly linked observations.

Primary and secondary enrolment counts may not be directly comparable because the student populations and year levels differ. In particular, the secondary-school analysis focuses on Years 7–9.

Enrolment counts also do not reveal students' motivations, language proficiency or the reasons behind changing language preferences. Similarly, school locations and counts do not establish which languages are available at individual schools.

Behind every enrolment number is a changing picture of the languages being learned in NSW classrooms.

About This Project

Beyond English — The Changing Languages of NSW Classrooms

Developed for FIT3179 Data Visualisation 2, Monash University, Semester 2, 2026.

This project explores how language enrolments in NSW government schools have changed between 2012 and 2024, and how the geographical distribution of schools provides additional educational context.

Methodology & Data Notes

Language enrolment data was analysed using enrolment totals, percentage changes, relative shares, historical averages and language rankings.

School location records were used to visualise school distributions and aggregate school counts by Local Government Area.

International school enrolments were compared with NSW government school language enrolments using an indexed time series, where 2012 = 100. This allows relative changes to be compared despite differences in enrolment totals.

Language enrolment data covers 2012–2024, with observations for 2022 unavailable. School location data represents 2026. The geographical visualisations provide contextual information and do not directly measure language availability at individual schools.

International school enrolments and language subject enrolments represent different student populations. Their trends should not be interpreted as evidence of a causal relationship.
